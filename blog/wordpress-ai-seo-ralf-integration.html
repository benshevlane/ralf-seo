Key takeaways

  • Ralf integrates with self-hosted WordPress (wordpress.org) via the REST API and an application password.
  • It publishes complete articles as drafts or live posts, including headings, internal links, and FAQ schema.
  • It injects and monitors structured data so AI engines can parse your content.
  • It does not redesign your theme, touch your plugins, or manage your hosting.
  • WordPress.com sites work only on Business plans or higher, where the REST API and custom code are available.

What the integration actually does

The WordPress AI SEO integration performs specific, repeatable actions on your site. Not vague optimizations. Concrete writes to your database through the official WordPress REST API.

Here's what Ralf does once connected:

  • Publishes articles. Ralf writes full posts and pushes them to your site as drafts or scheduled/published content, with your categories and tags applied.
  • Adds structured data. Each post gets Article and FAQPage schema so models like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude can extract quotable Q&A pairs. This matters because what makes content more likely to be cited by AI comes down partly to machine-readable structure.
  • Builds internal links. New articles link to relevant existing pages on your site using descriptive anchor text, which spreads authority across your content.
  • Monitors your AI visibility. Ralf tracks where your brand appears across major AI chatbots and flags citation gaps against competitors.

The short version: Ralf treats WordPress as the publishing destination for an automated AI search content programme, then measures whether that content earns citations.

How to connect Ralf to WordPress

Connecting takes a few minutes and needs admin access. Here are the real steps.

  1. Confirm you're on self-hosted WordPress (wordpress.org) or a WordPress.com Business plan. Both expose the REST API.
  2. Create an application password. In WordPress, go to Users, open your admin profile, scroll to Application Passwords, name it "Ralf", and generate it. Copy the string it shows once.
  3. Enter your site URL and credentials in Ralf. Paste your site address, admin username, and the application password. Ralf verifies the REST API connection.
  4. Set publishing preferences. Choose whether new articles arrive as drafts for review or publish automatically, and pick default categories.
  5. Approve the first article. Ralf runs a test publish so you can confirm formatting, schema, and internal links look right before the programme scales up.

Prerequisites worth checking: the REST API must not be blocked by a security plugin or firewall rule, and your user needs the Editor or Administrator role to publish.

What results should you expect?

Citations take weeks, not days. AI engines need to crawl, index, and then choose your content as a source, and that lag is real.

One verified example: Free Room Planner, a browser-based planning tool, started an automated Ralf content programme from a standing start. Ralf planned, wrote, and auto-published the articles, and 69 were live within about six weeks. Of those, 9 articles had been cited in AI answers, producing 33 citations in total. The fastest first citation took 13.6 days; the average was 43.3 days.

Be clear-eyed about that. 33 citations is a modest early result, and 60 of the 69 articles hadn't been cited yet at the measurement date. The client's product pages always earned far more citations than the new content — the 33 is what the fresh articles contributed on their own. Nothing here happens in a day or two.

Troubleshooting the WordPress connection

Most setup failures come down to a handful of causes. Here's what people actually hit.

"Connection failed" or 401 error. Usually a wrong or expired application password. Regenerate it and paste the full string including spaces. Confirm the username matches the account that created the password.

REST API blocked. Some security plugins (like certain firewall configs) disable or restrict /wp-json/. Whitelist Ralf's requests or temporarily relax the rule to test the connection.

Posts publish but schema is missing. An aggressive SEO plugin or theme sometimes strips injected structured data. Check for a plugin overriding head output, and test the page in a schema validator.

Articles appear as drafts when you expected them live. That's your publishing preference. Switch it to auto-publish in Ralf's settings.

Why is my content still not showing up in AI responses? Give it time. If weeks pass with zero movement, the issue is usually indexing or thin topical coverage, not the WordPress link itself.

Frequently Asked Questions

Quick answers to the questions WordPress owners ask before buying.

Does Ralf work with WordPress.com or only self-hosted WordPress?

Both, with a limit. Self-hosted WordPress (wordpress.org) works fully. WordPress.com works only on Business plans or higher, because lower tiers restrict the REST API and custom code that the integration relies on.

Will Ralf change my theme or break my existing plugins?

No. Ralf only writes content and structured data through the official API. It doesn't modify your theme, layout, or plugin settings. Your WooCommerce store, caching, and existing SEO plugin stay exactly as they are.

How is this different from a normal SEO plugin?

A traditional SEO plugin helps you write and configure content for Google search. Ralf focuses on answer engine optimization — getting your pages cited by AI chatbots. The difference between SEO and AEO is the target: blue links versus generated answers. Ralf publishes and monitors specifically for AI citation.

How long until my WordPress content gets cited by AI?

Expect weeks. In one verified client programme the fastest first citation took 13.6 days and the average was 43.3 days after publishing. Citations depend on crawling, indexing, and the model choosing your page as a source, so there's no same-day result.

Do I need technical skills to connect it?

No coding required. You generate an application password in your WordPress profile and paste it into Ralf. The hardest part is usually making sure a security plugin isn't blocking the REST API.

Can Ralf improve my existing old blog posts?

Limited. The integration is built to publish new, purpose-written articles rather than rewrite legacy posts in place. If your goal is a citation-ready content layer, that's the intended path.
