What does Otterly.ai actually do?

Otterly.ai is an AI search monitoring platform that tracks how often your brand appears in AI-generated answers, measures share of voice against competitors, and runs prompt research to see which queries surface you. It reports on citations, sentiment, and competitive context across supported AI engines.

It's a genuinely useful category leader for one job: telling you where you stand. Otterly has earned recognition as a G2 High Performer and gets referenced alongside tools like Profound, Peec AI, and AthenaHQ in the AI visibility space. If your question is "why is my website not showing up in AI responses?", Otterly helps you diagnose the problem.

What it doesn't do is fix it. That's the line that separates a monitoring tool from an execution platform.

What does Ralf do differently?

Ralf is an AI SEO automation platform that tracks your visibility across major AI engines, identifies citation and backlink gaps against competitors, and then does the work to close them. Where a monitor stops at the report, Ralf writes the content, restructures the pages, and runs the outreach.

Think of the difference between a smoke detector and a sprinkler system. Otterly tells you there's a fire. Ralf puts it out. Both matter — but only one of them saves you the fire brigade call.

This matters because AI citation mechanics reward content that actually exists. AI models decide which sources to reference based on relevance, structure, and how often credible pages corroborate a claim. You can watch your share of voice all day, but the number only moves when new, well-structured, well-cited content ships. Ralf is built to ship it.

Do the visibility numbers translate to business results?

This is the honest hard part of the whole category, and it deserves a straight answer. Monitoring tools can show rising mention counts without a matching rise in traffic or conversions. More AI mentions don't automatically equal more revenue — and some users of monitoring-only platforms have flagged exactly this disconnect over extended periods.

Ralf's answer is to tie visibility to shipped content you can actually point to. In the first six weeks running Ralf, Free Room Planner had 69 articles auto-published, and 9 of them earned citations in AI answers — 33 citations in total, with the fastest first citation landing 13.6 days after publishing.

Be clear-eyed about what that is: an early result from a standing start. 60 of those 69 articles hadn't been cited yet at the measurement date, and the client's product pages always earned more citations than the new content on its own. When Otterly is the better choice

Otterly wins in a few clear scenarios, and pretending otherwise would be dishonest.

  • You already have an execution team. If you've got writers, an SEO lead, and outreach capacity, you may only need the data layer. Otterly's monitoring is mature and does that job well.
  • You want a pure-play monitor. Some teams deliberately want tracking separated from execution to avoid conflict of interest between the tool measuring results and the tool producing them.
  • You're focused on brand sentiment research. If prompt research and share-of-voice tracking are your core need — not content production — a dedicated monitor fits neatly.

If your main question is "how do I track brand mentions across all major AI chatbots?" and you'll handle the fixes internally, a specialist monitoring tool is a sound pick.

When Ralf is the better choice

Ralf is the stronger fit when the bottleneck is execution, not information.

  • You don't have a content or outreach team. GTM leads, small SEO teams, and Shopify merchants who need the work done rather than another dashboard.
  • You want gaps closed, not just flagged. Ralf's citation-level analysis feeds directly into the content and outreach it produces.
  • You want one budget line, not a stack. Combining monitoring, content, and outreach in one platform beats stitching a monitor to freelancers.

Understanding the difference between SEO and AEO optimization helps here: traditional SEO chases blue links, while answer engine optimization is about becoming a source AI models quote. Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers to the questions buyers ask most when comparing Ralf and Otterly.

Is Ralf a direct replacement for Otterly?

Not exactly — they overlap on tracking but diverge on execution. Ralf covers the monitoring Otterly does, then adds content creation, site fixes, and outreach. If you need both diagnosis and doing in one tool, Ralf replaces the monitor plus whoever was acting on its reports.

How many AI engines does each tool track?

Ralf includes six engines in its base plan: ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, Perplexity, Google AI Overviews, and Copilot. Otterly covers a core set on its base subscription, with additional engines typically available through paid add-ons. Always confirm current coverage against your priority platforms.

Does more AI visibility actually drive traffic?

Not automatically. Rising mention counts don't guarantee more traffic or conversions — a known challenge across the whole category. That's why Ralf ties visibility to concrete, published content you can trace, rather than treating the mention number as the finish line.

Can either tool fix my citation gaps for me?

Otterly surfaces where gaps exist; you or your team act on them. Ralf identifies the gaps at URL level and then executes the fix — writing content, adjusting structure, and running backlink outreach automatically.

Which is cheaper for a small business?

Ralf starts at $49/mo with six engines and no per-engine surcharge. Otterly's cost depends on your tier and how many add-on engines you need. For an SMB that would otherwise pay separately for content and outreach, Ralf's bundled model usually works out lower overall.

What's the difference between monitoring and optimization tools?

A monitoring tool reports where you stand in AI answers. An optimization platform changes it. Otterly is firmly in the first camp; Ralf spans both. We compare the broader diagnostic-versus-execution split in our Screaming Frog comparison.
