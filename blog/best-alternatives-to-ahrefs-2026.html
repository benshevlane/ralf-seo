Key takeaways

  • Semrush and Moz remain the closest like-for-like replacements for traditional Ahrefs workflows.
  • SE Ranking, Mangools, and Ubersuggest win on price for freelancers and small teams.
  • Sistrix suits enterprise brands tracking long-term visibility trends.
  • Ralf targets a different problem: being found inside AI-generated answers, not just Google's ten blue links.
  • Most people leave Ahrefs over pricing, data accuracy gaps, or slow support — not because the core toolset is bad.

Why do people look for Ahrefs alternatives?

Most switchers leave for three reasons: cost, trust in the numbers, and support. Ahrefs is a genuinely powerful tool, but it isn't the right fit for everyone in 2026.

Pricing is the loudest complaint. Users describe the move from older grandfathered plans to newer credit-based pricing as poorly communicated, and the credits themselves as easy to burn through even with moderate use. For smaller operators, the entry tier can feel too restricted — several core tasks push you toward a more expensive plan.

Data accuracy is the second theme. Some users report meaningful gaps between the platform's traffic estimates and their actual analytics, and note that backlink detection can miss strong links while surfacing weaker ones. That matters when you're making decisions off the numbers.

Support is the third. When something goes wrong — a billing dispute, a technical fault — teams report slow responses and difficulty getting problems actually resolved. Separately, some long-time users feel the product's pace of improvement has slowed while rivals ship new workflow features. Those are two different frustrations, and they compound each other.

None of this makes Ahrefs a bad tool. It makes it a tool worth pressure-testing against alternatives before you renew.

Semrush

What it is: A comprehensive marketing platform covering SEO, paid search, content, and competitive research.

Best for: Teams that want one tool to run keyword research, rank tracking, site audits, and PPC together.

Standout strengths: Semrush's keyword and competitor database is one of the largest available, and its Position Tracking and Site Audit tools are mature. The content and PPC modules make it a genuine all-rounder rather than a pure backlink tool.

Limitations: It's priced at the higher end, and the sheer breadth can overwhelm newcomers. Extra seats and add-ons stack up quickly.

Pricing shape: Tiered monthly subscription in the mid-to-high range, with add-ons billed separately. For a deeper look, see our Semrush alternatives comparison.

Moz Pro

What it is: A long-established SEO suite known for approachable metrics and a clean interface.

Best for: Beginners and small teams who value clarity over raw data volume.

Standout strengths: Domain Authority remains a widely understood shorthand for site strength. The keyword explorer, rank tracker, and on-page recommendations are easy to act on without a steep learning curve. Moz's community and learning resources are excellent.

Limitations: Its index is smaller than Semrush's or Ahrefs', so backlink and keyword coverage can trail the leaders on very competitive research.

Pricing shape: Tiered subscription in the mid-range, with a free trial to test before committing.

SE Ranking

What it is: A flexible SEO platform that has grown into a serious full-suite competitor.

Best for: Agencies and lean teams that want strong features without top-tier pricing.

Standout strengths: SE Ranking lets you scale spend to what you actually use — you can adjust keyword tracking frequency and volumes rather than paying for a fixed block. Its white-label reporting is a favourite among agencies, and the feature set covers audits, tracking, and competitor research.

Limitations: Its historical data depth doesn't match the incumbents, and some advanced features feel less polished.

Pricing shape: Lower entry tiers with usage-based flexibility, generally cheaper than Semrush or Ahrefs for comparable work.

Mangools

What it is: A bundle of focused tools (KWFinder, SERPChecker, LinkMiner and more) built around simplicity.

Best for: Freelancers, bloggers, and small site owners who find enterprise suites overkill.

Standout strengths: Mangools is genuinely pleasant to use. Keyword difficulty scores are readable, the interface is fast, and the price is friendly. For straightforward keyword research and light backlink checks, it does the job without fuss.

Limitations: It isn't built for large-scale competitive intelligence or deep backlink auditing. Power users will outgrow it.

Pricing shape: Low, straightforward monthly or annual subscription with clear limits per tier.

Ubersuggest

What it is: A budget SEO tool from Neil Patel offering keyword research, site audits, and basic backlink data.

Best for: Solo marketers and startups who need core functionality at minimal cost.

Standout strengths: The headline draw is a one-time lifetime purchase option — rare in a subscription-heavy market. It covers the essentials well enough for early-stage sites and content planning.

Limitations: Data depth and accuracy sit below the premium tools, and heavy users report rate limits. It's a starting point, not an endgame.

Pricing shape: Cheap monthly subscription, or a one-time lifetime deal for the budget-conscious.

Sistrix

What it is: A visibility-focused platform popular in Europe, built around its Visibility Index.

Best for: Enterprise brands and SEO analysts tracking long-term ranking trends across markets.

Standout strengths: The Visibility Index gives a single, historical measure of how a domain's search presence moves over time — useful for spotting algorithm impacts and reporting to stakeholders. Its data is well regarded for accuracy in supported regions.

Limitations: It's modular, so costs grow as you add capabilities, and it's less of an all-in-one workflow tool than Semrush.

Pricing shape: Modular, higher-end pricing where you pay per module.

Ralf

What it is: Ralf is an AI-powered platform that tracks where a business appears across AI engines like ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude, then works to close the gaps.

Best for: Businesses that care less about Google rankings alone and more about being cited inside AI-generated answers — the emerging discipline of answer engine improvement (AEO).

Standout strengths: Traditional tools like Ahrefs measure Google. Ralf measures a different surface entirely. It monitors your brand's presence across major AI chatbots, compares your citation and backlink gaps against competitors, and executes fixes — content, site structure, and outreach — rather than just reporting problems. If your entire workflow revolves around Google SERP analysis, you'll likely run it alongside a traditional tool rather than instead of one. How do you choose the right Ahrefs alternative?

Start with the job you're hiring the tool to do. If you need classic Google SEO — keyword research, backlinks, rank tracking — Semrush or Moz map most closely to Ahrefs. If budget is the driver, SE Ranking, Mangools, or Ubersuggest deliver most of the value for far less.

But if your real problem is AI visibility — appearing in ChatGPT and Gemini answers — no amount of backlink data solves that directly. That's a separate strategy, and we cover the split in our guide on whether you need a separate approach for AI search versus Google.

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers to the questions buyers ask most when comparing Ahrefs alternatives in 2026.

What is the cheapest alternative to Ahrefs?

Ubersuggest and Mangools are typically the most affordable options, with Ubersuggest offering a one-time lifetime plan and Mangools a low monthly subscription. Both cover core keyword and backlink basics, though data depth is lighter than the premium suites.

Is there a free alternative to Ahrefs?

Several tools offer free tiers or trials, including Ubersuggest and Ahrefs' own limited free tools. Free versions cap data volume and features, so they suit hobby sites and early research rather than professional workflows.

What is the difference between SEO and AEO improvement?

SEO improves how you rank in traditional search engines like Google, while AEO (answer engine improvement) improves how likely AI models are to cite you in generated answers. AI engines choose sources based on relevance, authority, and structured content — so the tactics overlap but aren't identical.

Can Ahrefs track my visibility in ChatGPT?

Ahrefs is built for Google-style search metrics and doesn't natively track citations across AI chatbots. Purpose-built platforms like Ralf focus specifically on measuring and improving where your brand appears in ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude answers.

Which Ahrefs alternative is best for agencies?

SE Ranking and Semrush are strong agency choices for their white-label reporting and client management features. Agencies focused on AI search increasingly pair a traditional tool with an AEO platform to cover both search surfaces.

Why do people say Ahrefs is too expensive?

Many users point to newer credit-based pricing that replaced older plans, plus entry tiers that restrict core features. When comparable capabilities exist at lower price points elsewhere, some conclude the premium is no longer justified for their needs.
