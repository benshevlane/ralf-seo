Most comparisons treat this as a fight. It isn't. Google still handles vastly more queries than ChatGPT, Perplexity and Gemini combined. But the queries moving to AI assistants are often the high-value ones: "which tool should I use", "best X for a small business", "compare A and B". If you only measure rankings, you'll miss that shift entirely.

What is the difference between SEO and AEO optimization?

SEO optimizes to rank a page; AEO (answer engine optimization) optimizes to be quoted inside an answer. SEO wins position on a results page. AEO wins a mention when ChatGPT or Gemini synthesises a response. The tactics overlap, but the finish line is different.

Traditional SEO chases a click. You rank, someone sees your link, they visit. AEO often produces no click at all — the model reads your content, summarises it, and names you as a source. The value is exposure and trust, not always a session in your analytics. That's why zero-click behaviour, which SEO teams treat as loss, is baked into how AEO works.

How do AI models decide which sources to reference?

AI models reference sources that are clearly written, factually specific, well-structured, and corroborated across the web. They favour content that answers a question directly, uses concrete numbers, and is easy to parse into a quotable statement.

In practice, three things move the needle:

  • Direct answers. A model can lift a sentence like "A room planner is a browser-based tool that..." far more easily than a rambling paragraph. Clarity is citability.
  • Corroboration. If several trusted pages say the same thing, the model trusts it more. This is where backlinks and mentions still matter — they signal that a claim is real.
  • Structure. Headings phrased as questions, clean tables, and schema markup help engines extract and attribute your content correctly.

Google SEO taught us to write for a crawler that ranks. AEO asks you to write for a reader that reasons. The overlap is genuine quality; the difference is format.

Which strategy delivers better ROI for your business?

Google SEO delivers better ROI when your buyers search with clear commercial intent and click through to buy. ChatGPT optimization delivers better ROI when your buyers research, compare, and ask AI for recommendations before they ever hit a search bar.

Here's the honest version. If you sell something people Google by name — "emergency plumber near me", "buy running shoes" — traditional SEO and local listings still pay the bills. If you sell something people research — software, services, considered purchases — a growing share of that research now happens inside AI assistants, and being absent there costs you.

The smart move isn't picking one. It's knowing which queries in your category have migrated to AI, and covering those with AEO while defending your high-intent Google terms. Our breakdown of automated AI SEO versus manual consultant cost covers the budget side of that decision.

What does ChatGPT optimization actually look like in practice?

ChatGPT optimization means restructuring content so AI engines can extract, trust, and attribute it — then tracking whether they do. It's less about keyword density and more about factual clarity, question-led headings, and measurable citation gains.

We ran this on a real client to see how fast it moves. Free Room Planner, a browser-based floor planning tool, started an automated content programme from a standing start.

  • 69 articles were live within about six weeks.
  • 9 of those 69 articles were cited in AI answers.
  • 33 total citations of the new content over the period.
  • Fastest first citation: 13.6 days after publishing. Average: 43.3 days.

Be clear-eyed about that. 33 citations is a modest early result, not a viral outcome. Sixty of the 69 articles hadn't been cited yet, and the client's product pages always earned far more citations than any new blog post. The point isn't that content magic happened overnight — it's that fresh, well-structured content can start appearing in AI answers within weeks, which is faster than most traditional SEO timelines. Why is my website not showing up in AI responses?

Your website usually doesn't show up in AI responses because your content isn't structured for extraction, isn't corroborated enough to trust, or simply hasn't been crawled and indexed by the engines that feed the model. It's rarely one thing.

The common culprits:

  • No direct answers. Your pages bury the point under intros and setup. Models can't quote what they can't find fast.
  • Weak structure. No question headings, no schema, no clean tables. Extraction fails.
  • Thin corroboration. Nobody else references you, so the model has no reason to trust a lone claim.
  • No monitoring. You can't fix what you can't see. If you don't track mentions across ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity, you're guessing.

That last point matters more than people expect. Manual checking — typing prompts into three chatbots and eyeballing the results — falls apart fast, which is exactly why manual monitoring of your ChatGPT presence fails at scale.

You track brand mentions across AI chatbots using a platform that queries ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Perplexity on a schedule, records where and how your brand appears, and compares that against competitors. Doing it by hand doesn't scale past a handful of prompts.

This is the metric traditional SEO guides ignore entirely. Rankings tools track positions; they don't track whether an AI named you as a source. So which one wins?

Neither wins outright — and that's the useful answer. Google still owns scale and high-intent search. AI assistants own the research and recommendation layer that increasingly decides which brands make a shortlist. Kill your Google SEO and you lose today's traffic. Ignore AEO and you lose tomorrow's shortlist.

The practical balance for most businesses: keep your proven Google terms defended, then build structured, citable content for the research queries in your category and measure citation frequency the way you measure rankings. If doing that by hand sounds like a second full-time job, that's because it is — which is the whole reason automated AI search optimization exists.

Frequently Asked Questions

Short answers to the questions marketers ask most when weighing SEO against AI optimization.

Is ChatGPT optimization replacing traditional SEO?

No. It's adding a layer, not replacing one. Google still handles far more queries, and high-intent commercial searches still convert best through traditional rankings. ChatGPT optimization captures the research and recommendation queries that are shifting to AI assistants.

How long does it take to get cited by ChatGPT?

It varies widely. In one measured client programme, the fastest first citation came 13.6 days after publishing, with an average of 43.3 days. That's often quicker than traditional SEO rankings, but most articles take longer, and many aren't cited at all early on.

What makes content more likely to be cited by AI?

Direct answers, concrete numbers, question-led headings, clean structure, schema markup, and corroboration from other trusted sources. AI models quote what they can extract and verify easily. Vague, unstructured content rarely gets cited.

Do backlinks still matter for AI search?

Yes, indirectly. Backlinks and mentions signal that a claim is corroborated and a source is trusted, which influences whether a model references you. They matter for AEO for different reasons than they mattered for pure ranking.

Can one platform handle both SEO and AEO?

Some can cover the overlap — quality content, structure and technical health serve both. But tracking AI citations and mentions is a distinct capability most traditional SEO tools lack, so check that any platform actually monitors AI engines, not just Google rankings.
