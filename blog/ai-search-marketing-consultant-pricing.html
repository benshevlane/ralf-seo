What does an AI search marketing consultant actually do?

An AI search marketing consultant helps your business get cited in answers from ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and other AI engines, rather than just ranking on a Google results page. The work sits under a discipline called answer engine optimization, or AEO.

In practice, the job breaks into a few repeating tasks. They audit where you currently appear across AI chatbots. They compare your citation footprint against competitors. They fix your content and site structure so models can parse and quote you. And they run outreach to build the kind of third-party mentions that AI models treat as trust signals. Some also handle traditional SEO, paid media, or CRO alongside — but the AI-specific slice is what commands the premium right now.

What is the difference between SEO and AEO pricing?

SEO pricing pays for ranking on search results pages; AEO pricing pays for being the source an AI model quotes in its answer. The work overlaps, but the deliverables and measurement differ enough that the money follows a different logic.

Traditional SEO consultants often price against keyword rankings and organic traffic. AEO consultants price against citation share — how often your brand appears when someone asks ChatGPT or Gemini a question in your category. Because AEO tooling is newer and the talent pool is thinner, per-hour rates trend higher. But the retainer bands overlap heavily, so don't assume the AEO label alone justifies double the fee. Ask what they measure and how often they report it.

AI search marketing consultant pricing models explained

Most consultants offer one of four structures. Each suits a different buyer.

Hourly rates: $150 to $400 per hour

Hourly works for a bounded problem — a one-time audit, a technical schema review, or a second opinion on a strategy you already have. You'll see rates from roughly $150 for a generalist to $400 for a specialist with a track record of AI citations.

The risk with hourly is simple. You're buying time, not results. A consultant who bills by the hour has no structural reason to work fast. For anything ongoing, hourly gets expensive and hard to forecast. Use it for defined, finite tasks.

Monthly retainers: $3,000 to $8,000 per month

Retainers are the default for ongoing AEO work, and published 2026 figures put the band at $3,000 to $8,000 monthly for AI marketing consulting. This covers a rolling programme: monitoring, content production, structured data fixes, and outreach.

The question that matters is what fills those hours. A good retainer ships work every week — articles published, schema deployed, citations tracked. A weak one produces a monthly PDF and a call. Ask for the actual output cadence before you sign, not the list of "areas of focus."

Project-based and system builds: $15,000 to $35,000

A full AEO system build — content architecture, structured data across the whole site, a citation-tracking setup, and an initial outreach push — typically runs $15,000 to $35,000 as a one-off project. This suits businesses that want the foundation laid properly before moving to a lighter retainer.

The trade-off is the feedback loop. You spend big up front and wait to see whether the AI engines respond. Which brings us to timing.

Software plus service: $200 to $2,000 per month

The fourth model bundles a platform that automatically optimizes your site for AI search engines with the execution itself. Instead of paying a human by the hour to do the audit, write the content, and run outreach, you pay for a system that does the repeatable parts and reports the results. This is the band Ralf sits in, and it's usually the cheapest way to get consistent execution.

Why does AI SEO take so long to show results?

AI citations don't appear overnight, and any consultant who promises next-week results is either misinformed or lying. Content needs to be published, indexed, and then judged trustworthy enough by a model to quote — that process takes weeks, not days.

We have real data on this. For Free Room Planner, a free browser-based room planning tool, Ralf planned, wrote, and auto-published 69 articles between 20 June and 30 July 2026. By 29 July, 9 of those 69 articles had earned AI citations — 33 citations in total. The fastest first citation came 13.6 days after publishing; the average was 43.3 days.

Read those numbers honestly. This is an early result from a standing start. Sixty of the 69 articles hadn't been cited yet at the measurement date, and 33 citations is a modest contribution — the client's product pages always earned far more on their own. The point isn't that the content exploded. The point is that citations arrive on a delay measured in weeks, and your pricing decision should account for that lag. Paying $8,000 a month for the first two months and panicking about zero citations is a misread of the timeline, not a failure of the work.

What changes the price you pay?

The range is wide because a handful of factors move the number more than the consultant's job title does.

  • Your starting position. A site with clean structure and existing authority costs less to move than one starting from nothing.
  • Competition in your category. Getting cited in a crowded B2B software niche is harder — and pricier — than in a quiet local service.
  • Scope. AEO-only is cheaper than AEO plus paid media, CRO, and email built on Klaviyo or HubSpot.
  • Execution vs advice. A consultant who only advises is cheaper per month but leaves you doing the work. Done-for-you costs more but ships.
  • Reporting depth. Real-time citation tracking across ChatGPT, Gemini, and Claude costs more to run than a monthly manual check.

Consultant vs platform: where does the money go?

A human consultant charges mostly for labour; a platform charges mostly for a system that already exists. That single difference explains the price gap between a $6,000 retainer and a $500 subscription.

When you hire a consultant, you're funding the hours it takes them to audit, write, deploy schema, and chase mentions — one client at a time. When you use software built for this, the audit runs automatically, the content pipeline is templated, and the citation tracking is always on. The honest catch: a platform won't sit in your strategy meeting or handle the messy human parts of outreach relationships. Many businesses find the right answer is a platform for the repeatable execution and a few consultant hours for judgment calls. What should a small business budget?

Most small businesses don't need a $35,000 system build. They need consistent execution at a price that survives a slow quarter. If you're spending under $2,000 a month, a done-for-you platform will almost always ship more actual work than a consultant billing at that level.

Start by tracking where you appear now — you can't price a fix without knowing the gap. If your site isn't showing up in AI responses at all, the first job is diagnosis, not a big retainer. How do you avoid overpaying?

The biggest waste isn't a high rate — it's paying premium prices for vague deliverables. Protect yourself with three questions before you sign anything.

First: what ships each week, in writing? "Ongoing optimization" is not a deliverable. "Four articles published and schema deployed on 20 pages" is. Second: what exactly do you measure, and can I see it live? If the answer is a monthly summary, you're paying for reports, not results. Third: what happens in month one when there are no citations yet? A consultant who understands the 43-day average won't panic-sell you extras; one who doesn't understand it will.

Frequently asked questions

Quick answers to the pricing questions buyers ask most.

How much does an AI search marketing consultant cost per hour?

Hourly rates run from roughly $150 for a generalist to $400 for a specialist with a proven record of earning AI citations. Hourly billing suits one-off audits and second opinions, but it's an expensive and unpredictable way to fund ongoing work.

Is a monthly retainer or a platform cheaper for AEO?

A platform-plus-service model, typically $200 to $2,000 a month, is usually cheaper than a $3,000–$8,000 human retainer for the same execution volume. The trade-off is that a platform handles repeatable work well but won't replace human judgment on strategy and relationship-based outreach.

How long before I see AI citations?

Expect weeks, not days. In verified Ralf tracking for one client, the fastest first citation took 13.6 days and the average was 43.3 days after an article went live. Any promise of near-instant AI citations is a red flag.

Why is my website not showing up in AI responses?

Usually because your content isn't structured for models to parse, you lack the third-party mentions AI engines treat as trust signals, or your pages simply haven't been judged authoritative enough yet. Diagnosing which of these applies is the first step — and it should cost far less than a full retainer.

What's the difference between AEO and traditional SEO pricing?

SEO pricing pays for ranking on search results pages; AEO pricing pays for being the source an AI model quotes. AEO rates trend a little higher because the tooling and talent are newer, but the retainer bands overlap heavily, so the label alone shouldn't double your bill.

Do I still need traditional SEO if I invest in AEO?

In most cases, yes. The two overlap — clean site structure and quality content help both — but they measure success differently. Many businesses run them together rather than choosing one, and a good consultant or platform handles both without charging twice for the shared groundwork.
