So read this as an early signal, not a victory lap.

The situation before

Free Room Planner is a free, browser-based room and floor planning tool. The product worked. People used it. And the product pages already picked up AI citations because they answered a clear, high-intent question: where do I plan a room online for free?

What the site did not have was a content presence in AI answers. There was no library of articles feeding ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude the kind of specific, quotable guidance that generative engines pull from. If someone asked an AI model a broader planning question — not "give me a tool" but "how do I lay out a small bedroom" — the site had nothing in the running to be referenced.

That gap is common. A tool can be excellent and still be invisible for every query that isn't a direct match for the tool itself.

What Ralf did

Ralf planned, wrote and auto-published the article programme. No human wrote these pieces by hand. The cadence tells you most of what matters:

  1. 20 June 2026 — the programme started from zero. First article published.
  2. Through July — articles shipped continuously, each structured for AI extraction: direct-answer openings, question-style headings, clean FAQ blocks, and schema.
  3. 30 July 2026 — 69 articles live.

The structural choices weren't decoration. Answer engines lift content that states its answer plainly and early, which is why every piece led with a definition or a direct response rather than a warm-up. This is the "done-for-you" model in practice: the platform identifies the content and citation gaps, writes to fill them, and publishes on a schedule the client doesn't have to manage.

What this does NOT show

Be clear-eyed here, because the honest version is more useful than the flattering one.

  • It's six weeks. Content programmes compound over quarters, not weeks. This is the opening chapter.
  • The sample is small. 35 citations is modest. Don't let the phrase "cited in AI answers" imply scale it doesn't have.
  • Most articles are uncited. 59 of 69 have earned nothing yet. Some may never get traction; some may need time or refinement.
  • Product pages are excluded. The site's tool pages earn far more citations and did so before Ralf touched anything. The 35 is only the new content's contribution. Anyone claiming the content produced the site's total AI visibility would be wrong.

If you were hoping for a chart that shoots up and to the right, this isn't it. It's evidence that the machine works and starts producing early — nothing more, and nothing less.

Would this work for you?

Where this transfers: if you have a genuinely useful product or service and a pile of informational questions your audience asks that you're not answering anywhere, automated content can start closing that gap fast. The Free Room Planner case suggests early citations are achievable within weeks, not quarters, when the content is structured for extraction.

Where it doesn't: if your product itself is weak, no content programme fixes that. And if you expect 69 articles to each become a citation magnet, you'll be disappointed — a minority did the work here, which is normal. Content is a portfolio, not a guarantee per unit.

Frequently Asked Questions

A few things people ask when they see this result.

How many citations did the automated content actually get?

35 citations in total, across 10 of the 69 published articles, measured over 21 June – 29 July 2026 in Ralf's own AI-visibility tracking. That figure covers the new content only — it excludes the site's product pages, which earn more and always did.

Why haven't most of the articles been cited?

59 of the 69 articles had no citations yet as of 29 July 2026. That's expected in a six-week window. Some content takes longer to be discovered and referenced by AI models, some needs refining, and in any content portfolio a minority of pieces tends to do most of the heavy lifting.

How does an AI model decide which sources to reference?

Generative engines favour content that answers a question directly, early, and in extractable form — clear definitions, structured headings, and supporting schema. Content that buries its answer or hedges is harder to lift verbatim, which is why structure matters as much as the writing itself.

Is six weeks long enough to judge an AI-content programme?

No. Six weeks shows whether the mechanism works and how fast citations begin — here, the first appeared on day two. It does not show the scale a mature programme reaches. Content compounds over quarters, so this result is an early indicator rather than a final verdict.

What's the difference between the content citations and the site's overall AI visibility?

The 35 citations came from the new articles alone. Free Room Planner's product pages earned AI citations before Ralf published anything and continue to earn more than the content does. Presenting total site visibility as a content result would overstate what the programme produced.
